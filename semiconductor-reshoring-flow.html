Foreign direct investment in U.S. semiconductor capacity · 2020–2026

Where semiconductor reshoring landed

Chipmakers headquartered in Taiwan, South Korea, Germany and Japan have committed about $327 billion to new semiconductor capacity on American soil since 2020 — the largest greenfield foreign investment wave in U.S. history. It did not spread out. Nearly all of it landed in ten towns, and four fifths of it in one metro area.

What each dot is

One dot carries about $2 billion of announced capital, released from the home country of the investing firm in the month the commitment became public, and settling on the U.S. site receiving it. Ring size grows with the site total.

Coverage and method

Included: publicly announced capital commitments by foreign-headquartered semiconductor firms to U.S. manufacturing sites, 2020–2026, where both a dollar figure and a location were disclosed. Investment by U.S.-headquartered firms — Intel, Micron, Texas Instruments, GlobalFoundries, Wolfspeed — is out of scope.

Not included: smaller materials and equipment investments where no consolidated figure has been disclosed, among them SK Siltron (Bay City, MI), Shin-Etsu and SUMCO wafer operations, ASML (Wilton, CT) and Siltronic.

Capacity-share figures are published for 1990, 2020, 2022 and 2032; values between those anchors are interpolated. Announced commitments are revised often, and upward.
